A store where every piece says where it came from.

Products start as source records, and the record stays attached: the original fields, the source, the revision you are looking at. Nothing is invented on the way to the shelf.

Prices are simulated and labelled that way. Nothing is charged, shipped or delivered. What you take home is the record of owning it.

Right now: products across categories.

    Buying is simulated. What arrives is real: a private key, shown only to you.

    Enter it one group at a time. Each group loosens a hinge.

    Then the doors open, and the piece is yours.

    Dressed for the weather Jakarta actually has.

    Forecasts come from BMKG for your village code, about three days ahead. Rain and heat change which outfits are suggested, and the app tells you when a day is past what the forecast can see.

    Plan seven days at a time. Marking a look as worn keeps the plan, so the week stays honest about what you meant and what you did.

    Shared with friends, private until you say so.

    Profiles, wardrobes and friend lists each have their own setting, and they start closed. A look you share is a look, never the pieces: saving a friend's outfit adds it to your inspirations, not your wardrobe.

    Your wardrobe is waiting. Open Wearhouse, or browse the catalogue first.

    Wearhouse is a capstone project in Information Systems at Sampoerna University. The store is a sandbox: prices are simulated and nothing is charged, shipped or delivered. The demo catalogue is synthetic.

    Weather data: BMKG (Badan Meteorologi, Klimatologi, dan Geofisika). Screens on this page are the running app with a demo account.
